Stanford CS248B

Resources

References and learning tools for physics-based animation and simulation. General material comes first, followed by readings for each lecture, which are added after the lecture is given. For a specific offering's schedule, slides, and assignments, see the list of course offerings.

General

Free course notes and books

  • Andrew Witkin and David Baraff. Physically Based Modeling: Principles and Practice. SIGGRAPH 1997 course notes.
    The classic introduction, with short chapters on differential equations, particle dynamics, implicit methods, constrained dynamics, and rigid bodies.
  • Daniel Shiffman. The Nature of Code: Simulating Natural Systems with JavaScript. No Starch Press, 2024. Free to read online.
    All examples are in p5.js, the library we use. Chapters on vectors, forces, oscillation, particle systems, and physics libraries.
  • Justin Solomon. Numerical Algorithms. A K Peters/CRC Press, 2015. Free PDF from the author.
    Chapter 15 of the PDF, Ordinary Differential Equations, covers the integrators used in this course.
  • Theodore Kim and David Eberle. Dynamic Deformables: Implementation and Production Practicalities. SIGGRAPH 2020 and 2022 Courses (ACM: 2020, 2022).
    Notes and code for deformable simulation as practiced at Pixar.
  • Eftychios Sifakis and Jernej Barbič. FEM Simulation of 3D Deformable Solids: A Practitioner's Guide to Theory, Discretization and Model Reduction. SIGGRAPH 2012 Courses (ACM).
  • Robert Bridson and Matthias Müller-Fischer. Fluid Simulation for Computer Animation. SIGGRAPH 2007 course notes.
    Free notes that later grew into Bridson's book, listed below.

Books

  • Christer Ericson. Real-Time Collision Detection. CRC Press, 2004.
    The standard practical reference on bounding volumes, spatial partitioning, intersection tests, and robustness.
  • Robert Bridson. Fluid Simulation for Computer Graphics, 2nd edition. A K Peters/CRC Press, 2015.

Surveys and tutorials

On publisher sites; these may need Stanford library access.

  • Adam W. Bargteil and Tamar Shinar. An Introduction to Physics-Based Animation. SIGGRAPH 2019 Courses.
  • Andrew Nealen, Matthias Müller, Richard Keiser, Eddy Boxerman, and Mark Carlson. Physically Based Deformable Models in Computer Graphics. Computer Graphics Forum, 2006.
  • Jan Bender, Matthias Müller, Miguel A. Otaduy, Matthias Teschner, and Miles Macklin. A Survey on Position-Based Simulation Methods in Computer Graphics. Computer Graphics Forum, 2014.
  • Matthias Müller, Bruno Heidelberger, Marcus Hennix, and John Ratcliff. Position Based Dynamics. Journal of Visual Communication and Image Representation, 2007.

Websites, videos, and talks

  • Christopher Batty. Physics-Based Animation blog.
    Lists new papers by conference, such as SIGGRAPH and SIGGRAPH Asia, and catalogues people and software in the field. Good for browsing what is current in research.
  • Matthias Müller. Ten Minute Physics (YouTube).
    Short videos, each with a small JavaScript demo that runs in the browser.
  • Erin Catto. Box2D publications.
    GDC talks by the author of the Box2D physics engine, with slides and some source code, on integration, constraints, contact, GJK distance, continuous collision, and bounding volume hierarchies.

Programming tools

  • p5.js and its reference documentation.
  • OpenProcessing.
  • Shadertoy, co-founded by Inigo Quilez and Pol Jeremias, a site for creating and sharing real-time graphics programs. Many SDF and ray-marching examples run live in the browser.
  • Graphtoy, Inigo Quilez's browser tool for graphing functions. Handy for trying out SDF formulas and boolean combinations.

By Lecture (Fall 2026)

Lecture 2: Particle Systems and ODEs

  • Witkin and Baraff, Physically Based Modeling: the chapters "Differential Equation Basics", "Particle Dynamics", and "Implicit Methods".
  • Solomon, Numerical Algorithms: Chapter 15 of the PDF, Ordinary Differential Equations.
  • Uri M. Ascher and Linda R. Petzold. Computer Methods for Ordinary Differential Equations and Differential-Algebraic Equations. SIAM, 1998.
    Chapters 2 to 4 (On Problem Stability; Basic Methods, Basic Concepts; One-Step Methods) are the most relevant to this lecture.
  • Shiffman, The Nature of Code: Chapter 2 (Forces) and Chapter 4 (Particle Systems), in p5.js.
  • John Denker. Basic Properties of a Symplectic Integrator.
    A visual explanation of why a symplectic integrator behaves better than a non-symplectic one.
  • Erin Catto, "Numerical Integration" (GDC 2009) and "Numerical Methods" (GDC 2015), on the Box2D publications page.
  • William T. Reeves. Particle Systems: A Technique for Modeling a Class of Fuzzy Objects. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 1983.
  • Andrew P. Witkin and Paul S. Heckbert. Using Particles to Sample and Control Implicit Surfaces. SIGGRAPH '94.

Lecture 3: Resolving Particle Collisions

  • Robert Bridson, Ronald Fedkiw, and John Anderson. Robust Treatment of Collisions, Contact and Friction for Cloth Animation. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 2002.
  • Inigo Quilez on signed distance functions:
    • 2D primitives, with live demos of all of them in his Shadertoy playlist.
    • 3D primitives, with formulas for combining them, and a Shadertoy playlist of the primitives.
    • Ray marching SDFs.
  • John C. Hart. Sphere Tracing: A Geometric Method for the Antialiased Ray Tracing of Implicit Surfaces. The Visual Computer, 1996.
    The basis of the ray-marched ball collisions in P1.
  • Matthias Müller, Ten Minute Physics, How to write a pinball simulation (video, code).
    Ball-capsule collisions, collisions against boundaries made of line segments, and touch and mouse input. A useful companion to P1.

Lecture 4: Collision Detection I

  • Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection.
  • Real-Time Rendering, Object/Object Intersection.
    A grid of intersection tests for common shapes, pointing to code and references.
  • Jonathan Richard Shewchuk. Adaptive Precision Floating-Point Arithmetic and Fast Robust Geometric Predicates. Discrete & Computational Geometry, 1997.
    The exact orientation tests mentioned in lecture. Code and an overview.
  • Stefan Gottschalk, Ming C. Lin, and Dinesh Manocha. OBBTree: A Hierarchical Structure for Rapid Interference Detection. SIGGRAPH 96.
    The source of the 15-axis separating axis test for OBBs.
  • David Eberly. Dynamic Collision Detection using Oriented Bounding Boxes. Geometric Tools, 1999 (revised 2008). Free PDF.
  • J. T. Klosowski, M. Held, J. S. B. Mitchell, H. Sowizral, and K. Zikan. Efficient Collision Detection Using Bounding Volume Hierarchies of k-DOPs. IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 1998.
  • Gino van den Bergen. Efficient Collision Detection of Complex Deformable Models using AABB Trees. Journal of Graphics Tools, 1997.
  • Philip M. Hubbard. Approximating Polyhedra with Spheres for Time-Critical Collision Detection. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 1996.

Lecture 5: Collision Detection II

  • Jonathan D. Cohen, Ming C. Lin, Dinesh Manocha, and Madhav Ponamgi. I-COLLIDE: An Interactive and Exact Collision Detection System for Large-Scale Environments. Symposium on Interactive 3D Graphics, 1995.
    Sweep and prune.
  • David Baraff. Dynamic Simulation of Non-Penetrating Rigid Bodies. Ph.D. thesis, Cornell University, 1992 (Cornell CS Technical Report 92-1275). Listed on the author's page.
  • Scott Le Grand. Broad-Phase Collision Detection with CUDA. GPU Gems 3, Chapter 32. Free online from NVIDIA.
  • Matthias Teschner, Bruno Heidelberger, Matthias Müller, Danat Pomeranets, and Markus Gross. Optimized Spatial Hashing for Collision Detection of Deformable Objects. Vision, Modeling, and Visualization (VMV) 2003.
  • Matthias Müller, Ten Minute Physics episodes, each with a browser demo and PDF notes:
    • Spatial hashing: video, demo, notes.
    • Sweep and prune: video, demo, notes.
    • Bounding volume hierarchies with Morton codes: video, demo, notes.
  • Erin Catto, "Dynamic Bounding Volume Hierarchies" (GDC 2019), on the Box2D publications page.
  • M. Teschner, S. Kimmerle, B. Heidelberger, G. Zachmann, L. Raghupathi, A. Fuhrmann, M.-P. Cani, F. Faure, N. Magnenat-Thalmann, W. Strasser, and P. Volino. Collision Detection for Deformable Objects. Computer Graphics Forum, 2005.
    A survey.
  • Doug L. James and Dinesh K. Pai. BD-Tree: Output-Sensitive Collision Detection for Reduced Deformable Models. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 2004.
  • Danny M. Kaufman, Rasmus Tamstorf, Breannan Smith, Jean-Marie Aubry, and Eitan Grinspun. Adaptive Nonlinearity for Collisions in Complex Rod Assemblies. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 2014 (project page).
  • Huamin Wang. Defending Continuous Collision Detection against Errors. ACM Transactions on Graphics, 2014.
