About how Collider Maker works

Trick Shot bodies collide as a union of disks: the simulator checks each disk against the scene's signed distance field with one distance query at its center. Interactive manipulation of a simulation needs its derivatives, and contacts that jump abruptly from one part of a body to another make them discontinuous [1]; Trick Shot blends the contacts of neighboring disks smoothly (a smooth min) to avoid that. Collider Maker turns a picture into such a set of disks in four steps, and keeps the picture itself as the body's art. The disks are fitted on a copy of the picture scaled to 220 pixels on its longest side.

1. The shape mask

Each pixel is inside or outside the shape. With a transparent PNG, a pixel is inside if it is mostly opaque. Otherwise a pixel is "ink" if it is darker than the threshold. With Fill the inside of outlines on (the default), the background is flooded from the image border, and every pixel the flood cannot reach is inside, which fills in outline drawings. An outline with a gap lets the flood leak in: close the gap in the picture, or use a transparent PNG.

2. The distance transform

For every inside pixel, the tool computes the exact Euclidean distance D to the nearest outside pixel, so a disk centered there with radius D minus half a pixel fits inside the shape. It uses Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher's linear-time algorithm, which computes each row or column's distances as the lower envelope of parabolas and handles 2D as a sequence of these 1D passes [2].

3. Candidate centers on the medial axis

Good disk centers lie on the shape's medial axis (its skeleton): the centers of the maximal inscribed disks, which touch the boundary in two or more places, and which lie along the ridges of the distance map [3]. The tool keeps a simple pixel approximation of these ridges: a pixel is a candidate if its distance is at least that of both its neighbors along some direction (horizontal, vertical or diagonal), and its disk is at least the Smallest disk size. Keeping whole ridge lines, not just their peaks, matters for bumps like a bottle's base lobes, whose ridges rise into the body without peaks of their own.

4. Greedy covering

Disks are added one at a time: each step adds the candidate disk that covers the most still-uncovered shape pixels. It stops when it reaches the disk budget or the coverage goal, or when no disk would add more than 0.2% of the shape. This is the classic greedy heuristic for set cover; for covering a whole set, it is guaranteed to use at most a logarithmic factor more sets than the fewest possible [4]. (Here it only approximates that setting, since it stops early and chooses among the candidate disks.) Approximating shapes by spheres is a long-standing approach in graphics: sphere trees for time-critical collision detection [5], including trees built from approximations of the medial axis [6], and shape approximation with Sphere-Meshes [7]. Collider Maker is a simple 2D, single-level version of this idea.

What the views and numbers mean

Every disk fits inside the shape, so apart from pixel rounding the disks don't stick out past the picture. The price is that thin parts and the outline's rim are missed. Coverage errors shows, at the fitting resolution, missed shape pixels in red and disk pixels outside the shape in blue (almost always none, since the disks are inscribed). Trick Shot computes mass and spin inertia from the union of the disks, so missed parts make the body slightly lighter than its picture. Its smooth-min contact can also touch slightly before the closest disk does, by at most ln(N)/β for N disks, which is under 2% of the body's size for Trick Shot's characters.

Tips

  • A body rests on the lowest points of its disks. For a flat base, check that the bottom disks reach the same height; with too few or too large disks, a bumpy base loses its own disks and the body balances on one point.
  • Lower Smallest disk to reach thin parts (arms, antennae); raise Disks to keep them.
  • Fewer disks simulate faster: every disk is checked whenever the body is near an obstacle.
