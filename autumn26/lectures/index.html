Fundamentals of Computer Graphics:
Animation and Simulation

This page contains lecture slides for CS248B. The lecture list below is tentative and likely to change. Slides for each class will be made available shortly before class, along with a short summary of the topics it covers.

Lectures

Lecture 1: Introduction 9/22

Lecture 2: Particle Systems and ODEs 9/24

Introduces particle systems and their zeroth, first, and second order dynamics, from directly specified parametric motion through particle advection and gradient descent to Newton's equations of motion with simple forces such as gravity, drag, springs, and gravitation. Then develops ordinary differential equations for animation, covering reduction to first-order form, linearization to the model problem, and the accuracy and stability of forward Euler, backward Euler, midpoint, and symplectic Euler, including numerical stiffness and a comparison of integrators on the harmonic oscillator.

Lecture 3: Resolving Particle Collisions 9/29

Distinguishes collision detection from collision response, then develops particle contact models: penalty forces versus instantaneous impulses, the coefficient of restitution that controls bounciness, tangential friction, and how these resolve inside a symplectic Euler integrator with velocity filters. Applies signed-distance fields to proximity queries, covering 2D primitives, gradients and closest-point transforms, transformations and boolean combinations, and uses them in P1 Pinball for ray-marched ball collisions that catch multiple bounces per timestep and post-step projection that keeps the ball out of moving flippers.

Lecture 4: Collision Detection I 10/1

Frames collision detection and proximity queries around the cost of all-pairs testing, separating the broad phase that picks candidate pairs from the narrow phase that tests them, and discrete tests from continuous ones that solve for the first time of collision. Then works through the narrow phase: 2D orientation and segment-overlap predicates and their floating-point pitfalls, bounding volumes from spheres and AABBs through OBBs, k-DOPs and convex hulls, how each is fitted, and the separating axis theorem used to test them.

Lecture 5: Collision Detection II 10/6

Turns to the broad phase, which culls the pairs that cannot collide: sort and sweep on sorted interval lists, uniform grids and the bucketing that replaces sorting, spatial hashing for unbounded domains including which mod function to trust for negative indices, and adaptive structures such as quadtrees and kd-trees. Then develops bounding volume hierarchies, their top-down construction, recursive overlap queries, and the rebuild-versus-refit choice after deformation, closing on continuous collision detection with circle-circle and point-edge tests and the robust root-finding they demand.
